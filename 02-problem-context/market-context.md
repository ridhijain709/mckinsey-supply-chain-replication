## Why Q-Commerce Economics Are Hard

1. **Last-mile cost is structural:** ₹28–₹45/order in India — irrespective of GMV scale. Unlike traditional e-commerce, there is no "bulk shipment" efficiency in 10-minute delivery.

2. **Density is non-negotiable:** Below ~160–265 orders/day (depending on AOV tier), every dark store is loss-making regardless of how efficient operations are — because fixed costs cannot be absorbed.

3. **Assortment is constrained:** A dark store typically carries 3,000–5,000 SKUs vs. 50,000+ for a supermarket. This limits AOV ceiling and exposes the platform to substitution risk.

4. **Consumer price sensitivity:** India's grocery consumer is highly price-conscious. Delivery fees >₹40 materially hurt conversion; yet delivery costs ₹28–₹45/order.

---

## The Structural Insight

Q-Commerce in India is a **winner-take-most** market within micro-geographies (2km radius catchments). The platform that achieves density dominance in a catchment first — through faster delivery, better fill rates, and subscription lock-in — commands pricing power, lower rider costs (batching), and compounding repeat purchase rates.

**This is why density is not just an economic lever — it is a competitive moat.**
